What Could Still Trip You Up
Live coding tests. Knowing concepts ≠ typing them under pressure. Practice building small apps without looking things up.
System design questions. "How would you architect an app that does X?" Guides cover patterns but not end-to-end architecture thinking.
Your specific experience. They'll ask about projects you've built. Have 2-3 stories ready about problems you solved.

My Recommendation
Before interviewing:

Build one small app using everything — SwiftUI, Combine, REST API, offline caching. Even a simple one. Having recent code to discuss matters.
Practice explaining out loud. "What's an actor?" You know the answer, but can you say it clearly in 30 seconds?
Review the rapid-fire questions from Guide 1. Those are the warm-up questions interviewers love.
Skim Apple docs on anything you felt shaky on. The guides are condensed — sometimes seeing Apple's explanation helps it stick.


Bottom Line
After three guides + quizzes: You're interview-ready.
Not "fake it till you make it" ready — actually ready. You understand modern iOS development at a level where you can contribute on day one.
Job postings that say "expert" and "deep knowledge" are using recruiter language. What they actually need is someone who can build features, debug issues, and learn on the job. You'll be able to do that.
